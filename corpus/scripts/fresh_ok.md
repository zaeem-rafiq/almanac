# Six numbers to check before you file

Cold open: I redo this list every January, because five of these six move and one of them has
not moved in two years. If your plan is running on last year's numbers you are leaving money on
the table, and it is the easiest money you will ever pick up.

## Retirement

You can defer $24,500 into your 401(k) this year. That is your own money, out of your paycheck,
before tax. If you are 50 or older there is a catch-up of $8,000 on top of it, so the older saver
is working with a much bigger bucket than the headline number suggests.

Separately, the IRA limit is $7,500. Separate account, separate ceiling, and yes you can do both
in the same year if your income allows it.

One number people confuse with the deferral limit: the combined employee-plus-employer ceiling is
$72,000. That is a different limit doing a different job, and almost nobody hits it.

## Health

If you are on a self-only high deductible plan, the health savings account limit is $4,400.
Family coverage is $8,750. Both of those include whatever your employer puts in, which is the
part that catches people out in the last week of December.

## Tax

The standard deduction for a single filer is $16,100. If your itemized total is not comfortably
above that, you are taking the standard deduction and the rest of the exercise is academic.

The annual gift exclusion is $19,000 per recipient. Notably, that one did not move this year, so
if you have been carrying the same number in your head you happen to be right.

## Rates

For anyone shopping: the 30-year fixed average is 6.71%. Check it again the week you lock,
because that number moves every Thursday and mine is only as fresh as this script.
