# The three-account setup I'd use this year

If you are starting from scratch, you do not need a complicated plan. You need three accounts, in
this order, and about twenty minutes.

## 1. The 401(k), up to the match

Whatever your employer matches, capture all of it. This is not an investing decision and it does
not depend on rates or markets. It is a raise you have to opt into.

Say you make $50,000 and your plan matches the first 5%. That is $2,500 of somebody else's money
per year, and skipping it is the single most expensive habit in personal finance.

## 2. The IRA

Then open an IRA and fund it. The limit is $7,000 this year. Set it as a monthly automatic
transfer rather than a lump sum in April, because the April version is the one that quietly does
not happen.

If you assume a 10% return on that, the arithmetic gets fun fast — though I want to be clear that
10% is a number I picked to make the illustration readable, not a forecast.

## 3. The taxable brokerage

Everything past that goes in a regular brokerage account. No limits, no rules about withdrawals,
slightly worse tax treatment. That is the whole trade.

## One note if you are buying

The 30-year fixed average is 6.85%, so run the rent-versus-buy math with that and not with the
number you remember from a few years ago.

Not financial advice.
