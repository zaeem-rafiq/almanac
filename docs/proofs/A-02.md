**Proof 1** parses each `captions.srt` from disk with `srt.parse` (srt 3.5.3), counts words off the
parsed cue contents rather than the raw file, validates the four `meta.json` fields and that
`published_at` is ISO-8601, and asserts cues are monotonic and non-overlapping. Negative half:
`test_corpus_shape_fails_on_a_short_script`, `..._on_unparseable_captions`,
`..._on_empty_meta_field` each break a tmp copy of the corpus and require the proof to go red.

**Proof 2** additionally checks something the issue did not ask for and A-05 depends on: that every
`quote` is an exact substring of its source's normalised text. A label pointing at a quote that is
not in the corpus is an ungradeable ground-truth row. All 54 located. Negative half:
`test_claims_fail_on_an_unknown_entity_key`, `..._on_a_quote_that_is_not_in_the_corpus`,
`..._when_a_status_is_never_exercised`.

**Proof 3 is not a grep, and the difference matters.** A grep against a wordlist of known creator
names can only find what somebody thought to list, so it passes trivially — the same weak-negative
flaw A-01's review caught twice. This check inverts the burden: it extracts every capitalised
proper noun, cashtag, @handle, bare domain and all-caps acronym in the corpus and fails on
anything absent from an explicit allowlist of generic finance vocabulary (months, weekdays,
`IRA`/`HSA`/`IRS`/`Roth`/`Series`, first-person pronouns). An unknown proper noun is a failure by
default.

Two independent demonstrations that it can fail:

1. **Control fixture, run before the corpus scan every time.** The proof refuses to report PASS
   unless the detector first flags all four planted references in
   `"As Graham Stephan puts it, you should just buy $TSLA and read the breakdown at
   ramitsethi.com, or ask @themoneyguy about it."` — observed: 6 findings across
   `['acronym', 'cashtag', 'domain', 'handle', 'proper_noun']`.
2. **Planted into the real corpus.** `test_detector_fires_on_the_real_corpus_when_a_name_is_planted`
   appends `As Graham Stephan says, buy $VOO at vanguard.com.` to a copy of
   `corpus/scripts/fresh_ok.md` and asserts the whole proof goes red naming that file. This is the
   stronger of the two: it proves the *corpus scan path* can fail, not merely the fixture.

The detector earned its keep on the first run — it caught the string `Almanac` leaking into all
five `meta.json` descriptions and `Freddie Mac` / `PMMS` / `irs.gov` inside the provenance comments
of the two `corpus/scripts/*.md` files. Those comments were removed rather than exempted: a real
brand name hidden in an HTML comment would still reach A-04's extractor, so exempting comments
would have re-introduced exactly the evasion the check exists to close. Provenance now lives in
`docs/walkthroughs/A-02.md`, and the corpus contains only what a creator would have written.
